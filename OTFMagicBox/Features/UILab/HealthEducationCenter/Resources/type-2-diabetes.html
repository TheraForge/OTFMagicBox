Understanding Type 2 Diabetes

Type 2 diabetes is a long-term condition that affects how the body uses glucose for energy. Insulin normally helps glucose move from the bloodstream into cells. With Type 2 diabetes, cells do not respond to insulin as well as they should, and blood glucose can rise over time.

General education only. This article cannot diagnose diabetes and does not replace advice from a qualified health professional. Contact a clinician about symptoms or personal risk. Seek emergency help for severe or rapidly worsening symptoms.

Possible signs

Symptoms can develop slowly, and some people notice no symptoms. Possible signs include increased thirst or urination, unusual hunger or tiredness, blurred vision, and cuts or sores that heal slowly. These symptoms can have many causes, so testing by a health professional is important.

Risk and prevention context

Risk can be influenced by several factors, including prediabetes, family history, age, physical activity, body weight, and a history of gestational diabetes. Having a risk factor does not mean that a person has diabetes. A clinician can help assess risk and decide whether screening is appropriate.

Everyday care themes

  • Follow the monitoring and medication plan agreed with your care team.
  • Choose regular meals and eating patterns that fit your clinical and cultural needs.
  • Build physical activity gradually and ask for professional guidance when another condition affects what is safe.
  • Attend recommended checkups for blood glucose and overall health.
  • Ask what changes in symptoms or readings should prompt a call or urgent visit.

Questions for a care visit

  • What do my recent results mean?
  • How and when should I monitor at home?
  • What should I do if a medicine causes side effects?
  • Which food, activity, sleep, and stress goals are realistic for me?

When to seek help

Arrange medical advice if you notice possible symptoms, have concerns about your risk, or find it difficult to follow your current plan. Follow your personal emergency instructions and contact emergency services for severe symptoms or whenever you believe immediate care is needed.
